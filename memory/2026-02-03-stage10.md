## Physics Implemented

### Acoustic Wave Propagation
For a point source at position p_src with amplitude A at frequency f:
```
p(x,t) = (A / dist(x, p_src)) * cos(k * dist(x, p_src) - ωt + φ)
```
Where:
- k = 2π/λ (wavenumber)
- λ = c/f (wavelength, c = sound speed = 1524 m/s for water at 37°C)
- ω = 2πf (angular frequency)

### Standing Wave Formation
With two identical sources 180° out of phase at opposite ends:
- Constructive interference at antinodes (pressure maxima)
- Destructive interference at nodes (pressure minima)
- Spacing between nodes ≈ λ/2

### Phase Sweep (Acoustic Manipulation)
Additional phase offset: ωt + 2π * phase_sweep * t
- Effective phase velocity = medium_speed * phase_sweep / frequency
- Allows "moving" the standing wave pattern
- This is how acoustic radiation forces are controlled in practice

## Design Decisions

1. **Grid-based vs. analytical**: Used grid for visualization readiness (Stage 11)
2. **Spherical spreading loss**: Physically accurate, enables spatial pressure variations
3. **Bilinear interpolation**: Good balance between accuracy and speed
4. **Phase sweep as additive**: Separate parameter allows independent control
5. **Serialization**: JSON-compatible for transmission to renderer

## Lessons for Future Agents
- Standing waves at very low frequencies (0.02 Hz) have *very long wavelengths* (76 meters!)
- This means the pressure field is nearly uniform across the bath
- Spatial variations come from the 1/r term more than interference patterns
- Phase sweep is more powerful than frequency modulation for creating effects
